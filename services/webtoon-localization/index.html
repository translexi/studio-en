How a webtoon project runs

Four steps, from raw file to published episode.

  1. 01

    Source file

    We start from the highest-resolution original file. Every conversion loses a little quality, so the better the starting material, the less gets lost at the end.

  2. 02

    Clean the artwork

    Text is stripped from bubbles, sound effects are removed, and any artwork hidden under lettering gets redrawn — so it reads comfortably on a phone screen.

  3. 03

    Translation

    Dialogue, narration, and sound effects are translated by humans. What's preserved isn't just the meaning, but the feeling.

  4. 04

    Typesetting & lettering

    Text is set into the artwork without covering it, with type and spacing that keep the reading flow moving.

The difference between typesetting and lettering

Typesetting drops the translated text into bubbles and panels with the right font, size, and style. Lettering goes further: designing type that fits the comic's mood, adjusting alignment and spacing, then redrawing sound effects until they look like they were always part of the art.

Frequently asked

From which languages, into which languages?

Korean into English, French, and Spanish is the lane we run every day. Indonesian, Japanese, and other pairs are also available, depending on the title.

Is the translation done with AI?

No. All of the webtoon work is translated by humans and lettered by humans, from the first panel to the last. Webtoon readers can spot a machine-translated title instantly — and that's not a reputation we're willing to hand our clients.

Our title isn't published yet. How do you handle confidentiality?

We're used to holding unreleased material. An NDA can be signed before the first file moves, and file access is limited to the team working on that title alone.

Which file formats do you accept?

The highest-resolution originals — PSD, CLIP, TIFF, or raw PNG. The closer to the source, the less quality gets lost. If exports are all you have, send them anyway: our cleanup step exists precisely for that.

How do you keep hundreds of episodes consistent?

Every title gets its own style guide: each character's voice, locked terminology, type styles per bubble type. Letterers may change; the guide doesn't.
